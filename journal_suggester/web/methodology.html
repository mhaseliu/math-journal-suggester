About

An experiment in recommending math journals from a paper’s title and abstract using Qwen3-Embedding-8B and Kev-4B

How recommendations work

Qwen3-Embedding-8B embeds the title and abstract, then finds similar published papers by cosine similarity. Each journal is scored by its closest paper. The 20 closest journals and their supporting excerpts are then passed to a fine-tuned Kev-4B for reranking.

The five recommendations are numbered 1–5 in descending order of fine-tuned Kev’s scores among the 20 journals retrieved by Qwen. Supporting papers within each journal are ordered by embedding similarity.

Data collection

The audited corpus covers 95 journals and series published from 2016 through 2025. It contains 10,061 reference papers, plus separate sets of 1,000 validation and 1,000 test papers. Training papers come from the reference pool.

Publication metadata comes from Crossref and publisher records; available abstracts come from Crossref, OpenAlex, publishers, and arXiv, with identity checks before matching.

Sampling approximately follows indexed publication counts; validation and test each include at least one paper per journal.

Held-out test results

All three methods were evaluated on the same 1,000 test papers with identical Top 20 candidate lists. The epoch-2 checkpoint was selected using validation and fixed before this test.

Top 1, Top 3, and Top 5 accuracy measure whether a paper’s actual publication journal appears among the first one, three, or five suggestions. All 1,000 papers count, including the 150 whose actual journal was not retrieved.

MethodTop 1Top 3Top 5
Qwen retrieval21.1%40.6%51.4%

Fine-tuned Kev placed the actual journal among its first three suggestions for 724 papers, versus 406 for retrieval: a 31.8 percentage-point gain.

Validation results

All methods use the same separate set of 1,000 validation papers and natural candidate lists; missing the actual journal counts as a failure.

The fine-tuned result uses the selected epoch-2 checkpoint from 8,000 training papers: a 29.0 percentage-point gain in Top 3 over retrieval. These validation papers guided our choice of learning rate and checkpoint.

The actual journal reaches the Top 20 candidate list for 84.5% of validation papers; the untuned Qwen retriever misses the remaining 15.5%. Fine-tuning Qwen may improve this coverage.

IHÉS, Annals, Acta Mathematica, Inventiones, and JAMS account for just 105 of the 8,000 training papers and 18 of the 1,000 validation papers. For these 18 papers, the actual journal reached the Top 20 candidates in only 6 cases, and fine-tuned Kev placed it in the Top 3 in 1 case. Limited training coverage may contribute to weaker performance.

Learning-rate comparison

Each trial continued training released Kev’s existing LoRA adapter and decision head (warm-start fine-tuning). All trials used the same 1,000 training papers and seed, with two training epochs. We measured Top 3 accuracy on all 1,000 validation papers after each epoch.

Peak rateEpoch 1Epoch 2
1e-547.4%49.5%
2e-548.2%50.3%
4e-549.9%50.8%

We selected 4e-5 by validation Top 3 accuracy. It succeeded on five more papers than 2e-5 out of 1,000, a small difference that does not establish an optimal rate.

Validation loss (cross-entropy) penalizes low probability assigned to the actual journal; lower is better. From epoch 1 to epoch 2, loss decreased from 2.264 to 2.198 for 1e-5, 2.210 to 2.182 for 2e-5, and 2.167 to 2.162 for 4e-5. Loss uses the fixed 845 papers whose actual journal was retrieved; accuracy includes all 1,000. Completed trials took 16–20 minutes each, including validation.

8,000-paper training results

The expanded run started again from released Kev with 8,000 training papers, proportional journal quotas and shortages redistributed. There was no minimum-ten floor; journal counts ranged from 6 to 399.

Peak learning rate 4e-5; AdamW with a five-epoch OneCycle schedule; microbatch one, accumulation eight. Validation and saving occurred every half epoch. After at least two epochs, three checks without a meaningful loss improvement (0.01) or a new best Top 3 score triggered early stopping. Small loss improvements accumulated.

The run stopped at epoch 3.5. Epoch 2 had the highest validation Top 3 accuracy and was selected under the fixed rule, with earlier checkpoints preferred on ties. This is the model now used by the website.

All 8,000 requests passed independent checks, with no rejected or truncated examples. For 1,191 training papers, the target journal was inserted because retrieval missed it; validation and test never receive this insertion. A three-update pilot checked the gradients and confirmed that saving and reloading the checkpoint preserved its predictions.

The completed run passed the data, prediction and stopping-rule audit. Reloading the selected checkpoint reproduced its saved validation predictions exactly.
